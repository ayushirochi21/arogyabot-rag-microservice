9. CHIKUNGUNYA
Overview
Chikungunya is a re-emerging viral disease caused by Chikungunya virus (CHIKV). It is
primarily transmitted to humans through the bite of infected Aedes mosquitoes. The
disease is usually self-limiting and is rarely fatal. Its most characteristic clinical features
are sudden fever, severe joint pain and rash.
The disease can cause significant joint symptoms, and although mild joint
manifestations generally resolve within a few weeks, more severe joint symptoms may
persist for several months.
Key Facts
• Causative agent: Chikungunya virus (CHIKV).
• Virus type: Positive-sense, single-stranded RNA virus.
• Family: Togaviridae.
• Genus: Alphavirus.
• Main vector: Infected Aedes mosquitoes.
• Chikungunya is primarily transmitted through mosquito bites.
• Incubation period: Usually short; symptoms may begin approximately 48 hours
after the bite and may take up to 12 days.
• The disease is generally self-limiting and rarely fatal.
• Severe joint pain is one of the most characteristic features.
• Chikungunya can occur without obvious symptoms, particularly in some
children.
Transmission
The main transmission cycle is:
Infected person → Aedes mosquito → mosquito becomes infected → bite of another
person → Chikungunya infection
• Chikungunya virus is most commonly transmitted through the bite of an infected
Aedes mosquito.
• Vertical transmission is also possible.
• Viral levels in the blood are typically highest during the first two days of illness
and decline thereafter.
• Because Aedes mosquitoes can also transmit other arboviral infections, clinical
symptoms may overlap with diseases such as dengue.
Clinical Features and Symptoms
Chikungunya should be suspected when the characteristic combination of fever, rash
and joint pain occurs.
Common clinical features include:
• Sudden onset of fever
• Severe joint pain (arthralgia)
• Multiple painful joints, often affecting both sides of the body
• Joint pain involving the hands, wrists, ankles and feet
• Joint stiffness
• Joint swelling in some patients
• Muscle pain
• Back pain
• Shoulder discomfort
• Skin rash
• Chills, particularly with high fever
• General constitutional symptoms
The rash may initially appear as flushing over the face and trunk and may develop into
an erythematous skin eruption. It can involve the trunk, limbs, face, palms and soles.
Joint Symptoms
Joint involvement is a major feature of chikungunya.
• Joint pain may be polyarticular and migratory.
• Small joints of the hands, wrists, ankles and feet are commonly affected.
• Larger joints may also be involved.
• Severe pain can make walking difficult.
• Mild joint manifestations generally resolve within a few weeks.
• More severe joint symptoms may persist for months.
Rash and Skin Manifestations
Skin manifestations may begin with flushing of the face and trunk and progress to an
erythematous rash.
The rash may occur on:
• Trunk
• Arms and legs
• Face
• Palms
• Soles
The rash generally fades or may undergo desquamation as the illness resolves.
Complications
Chikungunya is generally self-limiting, but complications can occasionally occur.
• Meningoencephalitis can occur infrequently.
• Neurological complications are particularly important in newborns and people
with pre-existing medical conditions.
• Severe or prolonged joint manifestations may significantly affect mobility and
quality of life.
• Deaths are rare.
Diagnosis
Chikungunya should be suspected clinically when there is the characteristic
combination of:
Fever + Rash + Joint/Rheumatic Symptoms
Laboratory confirmation may be performed using:
• Chikungunya-specific IgM antibody testing
• Virus isolation
• PCR-based detection, particularly during the early stage of infection
• Molecular testing such as PCR can help identify active infection by detecting
viral genetic material.
NCDC's Centre for Arboviral and Zoonotic Diseases lists the following Chikungunya
diagnostic services:
• IgM ELISA
• IgM rapid diagnostic test
• IgG rapid diagnostic test
• PCR/qPCR for Chikungunya.
Treatment and Management
Chikungunya is generally self-limiting, and NCDC's material states that there is no
specific treatment for the infection.
Management therefore focuses on supportive care:
• Adequate rest
• Adequate hydration and supportive care
• Management of fever and joint symptoms
• Avoid excessive physical activity during severe joint symptoms
• Gentle movement and mild exercise may help improve stiffness and morning
joint pain.
• Persistent or severe joint symptoms require medical evaluation.
Treatment should be guided by a healthcare professional, particularly when symptoms
are severe, prolonged or atypical.
Prevention and Control
Because chikungunya is mosquito-borne, prevention focuses on avoiding mosquito
bites and controlling mosquito breeding.
Personal protection:
• Use mosquito repellents.
• Wear clothing that minimizes exposed skin.
• Use mosquito nets, particularly when resting during the daytime.
• Protect the home from mosquito entry where possible.
Mosquito control:
• Eliminate stagnant water.
• Remove containers and other places where mosquitoes can breed.
• Practise source reduction around homes and communities.
• Maintain environmental cleanliness.
• Support appropriate vector-control measures during outbreaks.
People suffering from chikungunya should also avoid mosquito exposure, because
mosquitoes can acquire the virus from an infected person and subsequently contribute
to transmission.
When to Seek Medical Attention
A person with suspected chikungunya should seek medical evaluation if:
• Fever is high or persistent.
• Joint pain is severe or progressively worsening.
• Symptoms continue for a prolonged period.
• There are neurological symptoms such as altered consciousness or seizures.
• The patient is a newborn or has significant underlying medical conditions.
• Symptoms are atypical or severe.
• There is concern for another illness such as dengue or another febrile infection.
Chikungunya: Quick Summary
Cause: Chikungunya virus
Spread: Infected Aedes mosquito bites
Incubation: Approximately 2–12 days
Main symptoms: Fever, severe joint pain, rash
Commonly affected joints: Hands, wrists, ankles and feet
Diagnosis: Clinical assessment + laboratory testing such as IgM and PCR
Treatment: Supportive care; no specific antiviral treatment
Prevention: Mosquito-bite protection + elimination of breeding sites
Prognosis: Usually self-limiting; joint symptoms may persist for months in some
patients

Welfare, Government of India. Manual of Zoonotic Diseases of Public Health
Importance (ZDPHI Manual 2025), Chapter 3: Arboviral Diseases – Chikungunya
