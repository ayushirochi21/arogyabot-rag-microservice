12.DENGUE FEVER
Overview
Dengue fever is an acute viral illness associated with fever and a range of symptoms
including nausea, vomiting, rash, muscle pain, headache, retro-orbital pain, joint pain
and, in some cases, hemorrhagic manifestations.
The ICMR STW is designed to help clinicians identify probable dengue, recognize
warning signs, identify severe dengue, investigate appropriately and manage
complications such as shock.
When to Suspect Dengue
Dengue should be suspected when a child:
• Has fever
• Has at least two of the following:
o Nausea
o Vomiting
o Rash
o Myalgia
o Headache
o Retro-orbital pain
o Arthralgia
o Hemorrhagic manifestations
• Lives in or has travelled to a dengue-endemic area.
Simple Recognition Rule
Fever + ≥2 compatible symptoms + exposure to a dengue-endemic area → Suspect
dengue
Symptoms
Common symptoms/signs include:
• Fever
• Nausea
• Vomiting
• Rash
• Muscle pain (myalgia)
• Headache
• Pain behind the eyes (retro-orbital pain)
• Joint pain (arthralgia)
• Hemorrhagic manifestations
These symptoms are the clinical criteria highlighted in the ICMR STW for suspecting
dengue.
Warning Signs
A patient with dengue should be carefully assessed for warning signs, including:
• Abdominal pain or tenderness
• Persistent vomiting
• Clinical fluid accumulation:
o Pleural effusion
o Ascites
• Mucosal bleeding, such as:
o Melena
o Epistaxis
o Gum bleeding
• Liver enlargement >2 cm
• Signs of shock, including:
o Weak, rapid pulse
o Pulse pressure <20 mmHg
o Hypotension
o Cold, clammy skin
o Restlessness
• Rising PCV with thrombocytopenia, including situations where clinical
symptoms may not yet be prominent.
Important
Dengue + warning signs → requires closer monitoring and appropriate medical
management.
Severe Dengue
The ICMR STW identifies severe dengue through serious complications such as:
1. Severe fluid accumulation
Particularly when associated with:
• Respiratory distress
2. Severe bleeding
Significant hemorrhagic manifestations may require urgent intervention.
3. Impaired consciousness
Altered or impaired consciousness is a serious sign requiring urgent medical attention.
Investigations
The ICMR STW lists investigations according to clinical requirements.
Essential investigations
• Hemoglobin (Hb)
• Total leukocyte count (TLC)
• Differential leukocyte count (DLC)
• Platelet count
• Packed cell volume (PCV)
• Tourniquet test
• NS1 antigen — ELISA
• Chest X-ray
• Liver function tests (LFT)
• Renal function tests (RFT)
• Creatine phosphokinase (CPK)
• Albumin
• Ultrasound abdomen
• Dengue IgM
Additional investigations when required
• Dengue PCR
• Echocardiography
• Central venous pressure (CVP) monitoring
• Ultrasound-guided assessment of IVC collapsibility for monitoring hypovolemia.
Important: Do not present all of these as tests that every dengue patient automatically
needs. The STW categorizes investigations as essential, desirable and optional,
depending on the clinical situation.
Treatment
Probable Dengue WITHOUT Warning Signs
The ICMR STW recommends:
• Symptomatic ambulatory treatment
• Paracetamol for fever
• Avoid NSAIDs
• Daily monitoring of:
o Clinical condition
o PCV
o Platelet count.
Dengue WITH Warning Signs
Patients with warning signs require closer clinical management and monitoring.
Particular attention should be given to:
• Fluid status
• Hemodynamic status
• PCV
• Platelet count
• Urine output
• Development of shock
• Respiratory distress
If the patient's condition deteriorates, management should progress according to the
shock/severe-dengue pathway.
Dengue With Shock
The ICMR workflow distinguishes between:
Compensated Shock
Features may include:
• Tachypnea
• Tachycardia
• Blood pressure may still be maintained/normotensive.
Hypotensive Shock
Features include:
• Tachypnea
• Tachycardia
• Hypotension
• Peripheral pulses may not be palpable.
Emergency Management
The STW directs clinicians to:
Assess airway, breathing and circulation → Start oxygen → Initiate appropriate fluid
resuscitation → Continuously monitor → Urgent referral to a facility with PICU
capability when required.
Fluid Management in Shock
The ICMR STW provides a stepwise fluid-management pathway based on clinical
response and monitoring.
The document specifies:
• Initial crystalloid/colloid bolus according to the shock pathway.
• Reassessment using parameters such as:
o Heart rate
o Respiratory rate
o Blood pressure
o PCV
o CVP where applicable
• Fluid infusion is progressively reduced when the patient improves.
• If there is no improvement, clinicians should assess for:
o Blood loss
o Acidosis
o Cardiac dysfunction
o Fluid overload
o Other causes of persistent shock.
For your awareness PDF, do NOT reproduce all the exact fluid rates unless your
template has enough space. The complete fluid algorithm is already available in the
official ICMR STW and is more appropriate for healthcare professionals than for a
general awareness chatbot.
Blood Products
The ICMR workflow provides indications for blood-product use.
Packed Red Cells
The STW lists situations including:
• Significant overt blood loss
• Refractory shock
• Other clinically significant blood-loss situations.
Platelets
The STW lists situations including:
• Prolonged shock
• Prophylactic platelet transfusion at very low platelet counts
• Systemic massive bleeding.
Fresh Frozen Plasma / Cryoprecipitate
May be considered when there is:
• Coagulopathy with bleeding.
Monitoring
During significant dengue illness, the ICMR workflow emphasizes monitoring of:
• Heart rate
• Respiratory rate
• Blood pressure
• PCV
• Platelet count
• Fluid status
• Clinical condition
• Urine output
• Respiratory status
In shock management, reassessment is performed repeatedly to determine whether the
patient is improving or requires escalation of treatment.
Discharge Criteria
The ICMR STW specifies that all of the following should be present:
Clinical
• No fever for 48 hours
• Improvement in overall clinical condition
• Improvement in general well-being
• Improving appetite
• Stable hemodynamic status
• Adequate urine output
• No significant respiratory distress
Laboratory
• Increasing trend in platelet count
• Stable haematocrit without intravenous fluids.
The workflow indicates that once the patient is stable, observation should continue and
discharge can be considered when the discharge criteria are fulfilled.
